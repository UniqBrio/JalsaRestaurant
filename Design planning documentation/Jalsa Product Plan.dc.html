Product & Design Plan

A working plan for the QR-first dine-in ordering platform, drawn from the two approved documents: the Application Requirements & Functional Scope and the Recommended Product Scope & MVP Priorities. It defines what we design, in what order, and which decisions are still open. Nothing here adds scope beyond those documents.

Product
QR-first dine-in ordering platform — web/PWA, not a full POS suite
Surfaces
Customer (mobile) · Captain/Waiter (mobile-tablet) · Owner/Admin (desktop) · Kitchen (KOT print / KDS fallback)
MVP mantra
Scan → Order → Kitchen → Add More → Request Payment → Tip → Pay → Invoice → Review → Reconcile
Visual system
Organic — cream ground, terracotta accent, sage second accent, Caprasimo over Figtree, pill controls, 16px radii

1. Assumptions I am working from

  • The requirements document is the baseline for design, development and UAT. Anything not in it is a change request, not a design decision.
  • The customer never marks an order paid. They submit a Payment Request; staff record closure and payment mode.
  • One table holds one bill; each order round produces its own KOT reference. Table and bill context survive every round.
  • Tips are not restaurant income. They are a separate ledger attributed to a captain or waiter.
  • Printer (TVS RP 3160 Gold), payment provider, and WhatsApp delivery are unvalidated dependencies. The design must work as a flow before any of the three is confirmed, and degrade gracefully if one is not available at launch.
  • Currency is ₹. Language is English for the MVP design; Tamil is designed for but not populated (content ownership unresolved).

3. Core model the screens must express

Every operational screen shows the same five identifiers, in the same place, in the same order: captain name, table number, bill number, waiter name, KOT number. This is the spine of the interface.

Worked example from the requirements: idli on KOT-001, biryani on KOT-002, pizza on KOT-003 — three kitchen tickets, one table, one bill, one customer session.

One bill across several tables — groups

A party of twenty sits at four tables and expects a single bill. The requirements assume one table to one bill, so this needs an explicit object rather than a workaround. The rule that changes is narrow: a bill belongs to one or more tables instead of exactly one. Everything else in the model stays as written.

DecisionHow it works
The group objectA group has its own number (G-07), a host table, a member-table list, one owning captain and exactly one bill. A single-table bill is simply a group of one — which is why nothing existing breaks.
ScanningEvery member table keeps its own QR. Scanning any of them lands in the same bill, with the screen naming both: "Table A7 · part of a group of 4 tables".
OrderingEach table orders independently on its own phone. Every round is still its own KOT.
KOTThe ticket gains one line: the table that ordered it. KOT-131 · from Table A7 · Bill B-1043 · Group G-07. The kitchen and the runner know exactly where the food goes; the accounting knows there is one bill.
Captain and tipsOne captain owns the group so the tip has a single owner. Other captains can still add items; the KOT records who did.
PaymentOne payment request, one payable amount, from any member table. The closure screen can show a per-table breakdown so the host can see who ate what, without splitting the bill.
ClosureClosing the group bill frees all member tables at once.

Four cases have to be designed, not discovered: joining a table that already has an open bill (its lines merge in, and the merge is logged); removing a table mid-service (its lines move to a fresh bill); a latecomer scanning a table nobody has added yet; and two tables requesting payment at the same time on one bill — the second sees that closure is already in progress.

On the owner floor the group reads as one entity: member tables share a badge and a tint, and the bill appears once in the orders list rather than four times.

4. The entrance queue — keeping it in order

One QR outside serves every guest, so the QR itself cannot hold a position. It is a plain link. The queue entry is created when the guest taps Join, not when they scan, and the server stamps that moment. Position is that timestamp, ordered to the millisecond, so two people scanning together still get two distinct places and nobody can jump by scanning faster.

QuestionHow it is answered
What holds their place?A token issued on tap — W-18 — plus a signed link stored on that phone. Reopening the link returns the same entry, with no account, no login and no name required.
How does staff identify the right party?The token carries a spoken pair alongside it, "W-18 · Amber Lotus", because a colour and a word are easier to call across a crowded doorway than four digits. The guest's screen shows the same pair at full size, so matching is visual rather than a conversation.
What stops the wrong party being seated?Each entry also has a four-digit confirm code. Staff sees it when seating; the guest shows it on their phone. It proves the token belongs to the people standing there, and it is the only thing typed in the whole flow.
Guests without a smartphoneStaff adds a walk-in from the waitlist in two taps. It receives a token, a pair and a code exactly like a scanned entry, and it takes its place by the same timestamp rule — so a paper queue and a phone queue are one list.
Duplicate entriesOne live entry per device. If a family scans three phones, staff sees three tokens with the same party size within seconds of each other and can merge them; the merge keeps the earliest timestamp so nobody is punished for it.
Not watching the screenA phone number is optional. Given one, the ready message also goes to WhatsApp. Without one, staff calls the pair aloud and the screen alert stands on its own.
Missed the callA table is held for five minutes. If the party does not appear, the entry returns to the front of the queue rather than the back, and only a second miss makes it a no-show. Both are logged with the wait length.

What the guest never has to do: give a name, give a number, keep a paper chit, or ask the counter where they stand.

5. Design principles for this build

  • Customer screens stay light, owner screens go dense. The customer sees a menu and a total; the owner sees a table.
  • One decision per screen on the customer path. Every tap that is not ordering, paying or tipping is a tap to remove.
  • Status in plain words — Pending, Accepted, Preparing, Ready, Served, Closed — never internal codes.
  • Food type is a visual primitive. Veg, non-veg and eggitarian marks read at a glance on cards, in cart lines, on KOTs and on the bill.
  • Money is never ambiguous. Subtotal, discount, tax and tip are separate lines wherever a total appears — the tip visibly inside the payable amount, visibly outside restaurant income.
  • Every destructive or financial action shows its author. Cancellations, discounts, closures and reprints name the user in the interface, not only in the log.
  • Organic applied restaurant-side: cream ground, terracotta for primary actions and totals, sage for confirmed and served states, washed photography for food imagery, pill controls at 44px minimum, rounded cards with air around them.

7. States and edge cases to design, not discover

  • Payment failed, pending, abandoned, or paid twice.
  • Item marked sold out while it sits in a customer's cart.
  • Cancellation requested after preparation has started.
  • Printer unreachable — the order must still exist server-side, with a visible print-failed state and a reprint path.
  • Customer requests payment, then orders again from the upsell strip.
  • Two people at one table ordering from the same bill on separate phones.
  • Captain reassigned mid-bill; tip attribution when staff changes.
  • QR scanned for an already-closed bill, or a table with no open bill.
  • WhatsApp number refused or invalid.

8. Dependencies that shape the design

These are not design choices; they change what the screens can promise. The design assumes each may be unavailable at launch and provides a fallback.

DependencyDesign consequence
TVS RP 3160 Gold printingIf browser-to-printer routing fails, the KDS fallback becomes a launch screen instead of a Phase 2 item
Payment provider not yet chosenPayment-request UX is designed independent of vendor; the pay action is a single swappable step with its own status screens
WhatsApp invoice deliveryInvoice screen designed with a download/on-screen path first, WhatsApp send as an additive action
Google review measurementReport shows link clicks and redirects only; the interface never claims a submitted-review count
GST configurationTax is a configurable line on the bill, not a hard-coded rate; the 5% figure in the reference is treated as unconfirmed

9. Out of scope for this design round

Per the scope guardrails: inventory and recipes, customer CRM, feedback module, native captain app, multi-terminal sync, offline-first operation, advanced analytics, kiosk, waiter-calling devices, takeaway tokens, reservations, digital menu boards, purchase and vendor management, loyalty, campaigns, multi-outlet management and aggregator integrations. These are roadmap items. Designing them now would imply commitment.
