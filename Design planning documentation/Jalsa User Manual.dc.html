User Manual

How to run the ordering system, written for the people who will actually use it. Section 2 is for captains and waiters and can be handed over on its own. Sections 3 to 8 are for Javeed and whoever else has owner access.

Two rules that explain most of the software. A guest never marks a bill paid — they send a payment request, and a member of staff records the closure against their own name. And a bill can cover more than one table, so a party of twenty across four tables is still one bill and one payment.

Section one

What the guest sees

Worth reading first, because everything staff do downstream is a response to something a guest tapped.

A guest scans the QR on the table. There is no app to install and nothing to sign into. They get a welcome screen that greets them by time of day, names their captain and waiter, and shows tonight's offers, then the menu.

Ordering

They browse by course, filter by veg, non-veg or egg — any combination — or search. Adding to the cart charges nothing; the order only reaches the kitchen when they tap send, and they can review quantities and add a note for the kitchen first.

Each time they send, that round becomes its own kitchen ticket with its own number, all on the same bill. A table can order five times in an evening and still get one bill at the end.

Asking for things

The ⋯ button offers water, a sealed water bottle, a call for the captain, extra plates or cutlery, and a parcel for what is left. They can also ask for a particular member of staff by name. Requests appear on the captain's phone and the owner's dashboard at once.

Paying

When they are done they tap request payment. That is not a payment — it tells staff the table is ready to settle, and it releases the table into clearing so the waiter knows to reset it. The guest then sees an offer strip, a tip step, and the payment action. Tips go to the floor team and are tracked separately from the restaurant's takings.

If the guest closes the browser nothing is lost. The QR is a fixed address for the table, so rescanning finds the open bill and puts them back where they were. After the bill closes they keep seeing the receipt and review prompt for a window you set — 15 minutes by default — and after that the same QR starts fresh for the next party.

Section two

For captains and waiters

Hand this section over on its own. Everything below happens on a phone.

Signing in

  1. Open the staff link and enter your four-digit PIN.
  2. Change it the first time you sign in.
  3. Sign out at the end of your shift.

Your PIN is you. Every order you place, every quantity you change, every cancellation and every payment you record is stored against your name. Do not share it, and do not work on someone else's session — if a mistake is investigated, the log names whoever was signed in.

Your five screens

TablesYour assigned tables and what state each is in. Tap one to open it.
ReadyRounds the kitchen has finished, oldest first. This is the screen to keep glancing at.
KOTsEvery ticket for your tables, and whether it printed. Reprint from here.
RequestsWater, bottles, plates, parcels, call-captain — grouped, with a count.
MeYour tables, your tips so far, what you have access to, sign out.

Table states, in plain words

FreeNobody seated. Ready for the next party.
OrderingGuests are seated and looking at the menu. Nothing sent yet.
In the kitchenAt least one round is being cooked.
ReadySomething is waiting to be carried out. Act on this one.
ServedEverything sent so far is on the table.
Payment requestedThe guests want to settle. Owner or cashier records it.
ClearingPaid or settling. Reset the table, then it goes back to free.

Taking an order at the table

  1. Open the table from Tables.
  2. Tap add items and pick from the same menu the guests see. Anything the kitchen has run out of is already hidden.
  3. Send it. The ticket prints with your name on it.

The guests can keep ordering from their own phones at the same time. Both routes land on one bill, and each round records who sent it.

Carrying food out

  1. Watch Ready. The oldest round sits at the top.
  2. Take it out, then tap it to mark it served.
  3. That unlocks the hearts on the guests' phones — if they heart a dish, they are offered it as a parcel to take home, which becomes a separate takeaway bill.

Changing or cancelling something

  1. Open the table, find the round, tap the item.
  2. Change the quantity, or cancel it.
  3. Give a reason. This is not optional — it is what protects you when the figures are checked later.

Before the kitchen starts, you can cancel yourself. Once cooking has begun it needs the owner, because the food has already been made and someone has to decide who absorbs it.

Requests from tables

Requests arrive grouped by type with a count, so five tables wanting water read as one line rather than five alerts. Deal with them oldest first and mark each done — that clears it from the owner's dashboard too. If you clear one by mistake, undo is on the confirmation for a few seconds.

When a table wants to pay

You will see the table move to payment requested. Take the bill over. Payment itself is recorded by the owner or cashier, and once it is, the table moves to clearing — reset it and it returns to free.

Section three

Opening and closing the day

Ten minutes at the start of service, five at the end. Day setup.

Before service

  1. Open Day setup. If today looks like yesterday, use the same-as-yesterday shortcut and adjust.
  2. Mark who is in. Anyone not marked in cannot be assigned a table and will not appear in tip settlement.
  3. Check the machines. If a printer shows as unreachable, deal with it now — mid-service is the worst time to find out.
  4. Add a notice if there is something the floor needs to know. It appears on every staff phone.
  5. Check the menu for anything you have run out of and close it. It disappears from every guest's phone within seconds.

Day setup is for today. Settings is for always. Who is working, which machines are up and tonight's notice belong in Day setup. Opening hours, tax rates, table names and printer configuration belong in Settings and should rarely be touched.

During service

The dashboard is the screen to leave open. Every figure on it is clickable and lands on the detail behind it. Watch three things: bills awaiting closure, open table requests, and any alert about a printer or a failed payment.

At the end of the night

  1. Close any bills still awaiting closure. A bill left open never reaches the reports.
  2. Enter the day's expenses while you remember them.
  3. Settle tips. The figure is per person and is not part of the restaurant's income.
  4. Open Reports › Final report for the day and check it against the cash drawer.
Section four

The menu

Menu. Search by dish, category or food type.

Adding or changing an item

  1. Tap add item, or edit on an existing row.
  2. Fill in name, price, category and food type. For category you can search the existing ones or type a new one and add it without leaving the form.
  3. Add a photograph. Guests order more of what they can see.
  4. Optionally set serving windows — idly from 7 to 11 in the morning and again from 7 in the evening, biryani from noon.
  5. Optionally uncheck days. All seven are on by default.

Outside its window or on an unchecked day, a dish is simply not shown, so nobody orders idly at three in the afternoon.

Running out of something

Flip the switch on the row. The dish leaves every guest's phone within seconds, defaulting to tonight only and back tomorrow at open. Captains see the same switch on their menu screen.

For a longer stop — the master is off for three days — open the item and choose a duration under availability: tonight only, three days, a week, or until you switch it back. The row then reads "3 days · back Tue 9 Sep", and everything currently off appears in one card with a put-back button.

Section five

Money — payments, discounts, tips

Recording a payment

  1. Open Payments. Bills awaiting closure are at the top.
  2. Open one. Check the itemised total — food, discount, GST and tip are separate lines.
  3. Apply a discount if there is one. Enter a percentage or an amount; the other is calculated as you type.
  4. Pick the payment mode and record any reference number.
  5. Record it. Your name goes on the closure.

Discounts are logged with your name, the amount and the time. That is deliberate. If the day's takings look wrong, the discount log is the first place anyone looks.

A party across several tables

Open any of their bills, then the tables cell in the identifier strip, and add the other tables. From then on it is one bill: each table keeps its own QR and orders on its own phone, every ticket carries the table it came from so the food goes to the right place, and payment is requested once from any of them. Closing it frees all the tables together.

The closure screen shows what each table ordered, so the host can see who ate what — it is still one bill and one payment.

Tips

Tips are not restaurant income and the software never treats them as such. They sit in their own ledger, attributed to the captain who owned the table, with a settlement state. Tips shows the total and the per-person split, and lets you record a settlement.

Expenses

Expenses. Date, category, amount, note. Categories work like the menu ones — search the existing or add a new one inline. Rows can be edited or deleted, each with a reason, because these figures feed the final report.

Section six

Staff and access

Staff. Adding somebody runs through to them being able to sign in.

Adding a person

  1. Add staff. Enter name, mobile and role; a new role can be added inline if none fits.
  2. Fill in the employment record — designation, department, date of joining, salary, gender, employment type. These are what the offer letter, experience certificate and payslip merge in, so filling them now means never being asked twice.
  3. Switch on staff app access if they need it.
  4. Generate a PIN. It is shown once — copy it, or send it on WhatsApp.
  5. Set module access. Apply the preset for their role, then adjust anything by hand. Confidential permissions are marked.

Paperwork

Paperwork on any staff row opens their offer letter, experience certificate or payslip, filled from the employment record and the restaurant identity in Settings. Anything not yet filled prints as a highlighted placeholder, so nothing gets signed blank.

Removing someone

Remove on their row, with a reason. Their PIN stops working and they leave Day setup, tip settlement and table assignment — but every bill, ticket and audit entry they touched keeps their name. If they are only away for a while, switch off their app access instead. You cannot remove yourself.

Section seven

Reports and the audit trail

Reports

One date range at the top governs every panel — presets, or custom from and to dates. Everything exports what is on screen, with the current range and filters.

SalesRevenue by day, top and slow sellers, payment mix, category performance
Purchases & expensesEverything entered in Expenses, grouped by category
Final reportIncome against expenses for the range, with the closing position. The one to check nightly.
All ordersEvery order, one row each, for reconciliation and disputes
UpliftRevenue the system itself created — repeat rounds, upsells, tips, parcels

The audit trail

Audit log holds date, time, what changed, which bill or table, and who. Filter by type, search across every column, sort any column. Use it when a figure looks wrong: it will tell you who deleted the order, changed the quantity, removed the item or applied the discount, and when.

Settings, printing, and when things break

Customer engagementReview link, photo prompt, WhatsApp settings, phone number

Seat counts matter more than they look. The waitlist reads them when suggesting where to put a party. A wrong number sends four people to a two-seater.

The waitlist on a busy night

  1. Set the quoted wait and make sure the queue is open.
  2. Guests scan the entrance QR and pick their party size. Position comes from the second they tap join, so simultaneous scans get separate places.
  3. Someone without a phone gets added as a walk-in — optionally with a number.
  4. Each row shows how that party can be reached. If it says no phone, the pair name called at the door is the only way, so Notify is not offered.
  5. Seat opens the suggestions that actually fit their size, tightest first. Bigger parties get table combinations on one bill.

When a printer stops

The order is never lost. It exists on the server, the failure shows on the record with a retry, and station tickets fall back to the main kitchen printer with the station name in the header. Reprints are marked as reprints across the top — that is what stops a cook making the same round twice.

When a payment fails

Nothing is charged and the bill stays open. The guest sees that plainly and can try again or pay the captain directly. If they pay at the table, record it as cash or UPI in the normal way.

Three things to check first when something looks wrong. Is the bill actually closed — an open bill never reaches the reports. Is the person signed in as themselves — the log names whoever was. And has the item or table been switched off rather than deleted — deactivated things keep their history and can be switched back.

One last thing. Almost every phrase a guest reads, and every optional button they can tap, is a setting in this software. If something on a guest's phone reads wrongly for Jalsa, change it under Settings rather than living with it.
