# Track E — Brainstorm

> For when there is no clear next action yet. **Thinking only. No code, no gates, no file
> edits** until the decision routes back into a normal track.

**TOPIC:** `<the question or the discomfort>`

---

## E1 — Ground in what is actually true

Read the real system and the registers first. A brainstorm anchored to what the app *actually*
does is worth several anchored to what everyone remembers it doing.

State the current behaviour in a short paragraph before proposing anything.

---

## E2 — Separate the question types

Most muddled discussions are three different questions wearing one coat. Sort them:

| Type | Belongs in |
|---|---|
| **A defect** — it does not do what it says | Track C |
| **A rule question** — it works as built, but the rule is wrong | A decision, then Track B |
| **A product question** — should this exist at all? | This discussion |
| **A process question** — how did this reach us? | Track F |

Naming the type usually resolves half the discussion on its own.

---

## E3 — Two to four genuinely different options

Not one option and two strawmen. Each option gets:

- What it is, concretely enough to argue with.
- What it costs — effort, complexity, ongoing maintenance.
- What it forecloses. Every choice removes options; say which.
- Who it is worse for. If nobody, you have not found the trade-off yet.

Include "do nothing" as a real option, with its own cost. Sometimes it wins.

---

## E4 — Recommend, then converge

Give a recommendation with reasoning. A brainstorm that ends in "it depends" has moved the
problem, not advanced it.

**Deliverable — a Decision Summary:**

```
DECISION: <what was decided, or "deferred pending X">
REASONING: <why, in two or three sentences>
REJECTED: <the options not taken, and the reason each lost>
NEXT STEP: <exactly one of:>
  - a drafted Track A/B/C request, ready to run
  - a rule recorded in a register
  - "no change" — stated deliberately, with the reason
```

Record rejected options. Six months later someone will propose one of them again, and the
reason it lost is the most valuable line in this document.
